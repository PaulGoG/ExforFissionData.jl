Observables

An abscissa is a list, because it is a joint index: ["mass"], or ["mass", "total_kinetic_energy"] for ν(A, TKE). A joint abscissa is therefore no separate vocabulary item, and the directory it writes to is the ordinate, vs, then the abscissa symbols — nu_vs_A_TKE, Y_vs_A, spectrum_maxwellian_ratio_vs_E.

The eight abscissae the archive can be asked for are ["mass"], ["product_mass"], ["charge"], ["neutron_energy"], ["total_kinetic_energy"], ["charge", "product_mass"], ["mass", "total_kinetic_energy"] and ["neutron_number"], and the empty list [] of a quantity per fission, which only the prompt-γ ordinates take and whose directory is the ordinate symbol alone: M_gamma_bar, E_gamma_tot, eps_gamma.

Each ordinate belongs to one EXFOR quantity code: yield to FY, the multiplicities to NU, the kinetic energies to E, the spectra to MFQ, the prompt-γ multiplicities to FY and the prompt-γ energies to E. The configuration does not name the code; it is read from the ordinate. The quantity decides which datasets the archive offers at all, and several ordinates impose no tags of their own, so a wrongly paired quantity would retrieve a different observable under the requested name.

Not every combination exists in the archive. ν(TKE), for instance, is reported as a pair quantity, so multiplicity_per_fission against ["total_kinetic_energy"] returns data where multiplicity returns none.

The width of the TKE distribution

σ_TKE(A), the standard deviation of the pre-neutron total kinetic energy at fixed pre-neutron mass, has no code in EXFOR. Dictionaries 34 (modifiers) and 236 (quantities) of the current dictionary transmission carry no quantity or modifier for a width, dispersion, variance, standard deviation or FWHM of a kinetic-energy distribution, and the Formats Manual leaves such a value to a MISC column explained in MISC-COL free text. The archive fills it inconsistently — a standard deviation, a variance in MeV², a FWHM, a "dispersion", of the TKE or of one fragment's energy — and once with no width at all.

The width is therefore read only from the columns a configuration names, one [[width]] table per dataset with the column, what it holds and whose energy it is (see Configurations); never from the free text, and never from the datum. A named dataset is selected as the mean TKE of its entry is, and its width converted to σ of the TKE: a variance by its square root, a FWHM by 2√(2 ln 2), a half width by √(2 ln 2), and the width of the energy of a fragment of mass A by A₀/(A₀ − A), which pre-neutron momentum conservation makes exact at fixed mass. The run record gives, per dataset, the column, the conversion, the mass range, and the MISC-COL text of the subentry quoted.

Where a publication settles what a column holds against its label, the record says so:

  • 23012005 and 23012006 (Nishio 1995, doi:10.1080/18811248.1995.9731725) are labelled FWHM, and hold half of it: the paper's Fig. 6 bars are the FWHM, and measured on the figure they are 2.00 to 2.10 times the columns. Converted, the two should agree exactly at fixed mass; they differ by 29 to 32 %, which the record states without reconciling it.
  • The width column of 12709004 (Weber 1981, doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.23.2100), labelled a standard deviation, is refused even when named: it runs from 47 to 94 where Fig. 3c of the paper plots σ(TKE) from 15 to 10.5 MeV, following the plot as an uncalibrated, inverted digitisation.
  • The widths of 22780003 (Hambsch 1997) at A = 180 and 181, 0 and 1.87 MeV, are not written: no measurement has them, and a consumer would take them at face value.
  • The dispersion of 240-Pu(sf) in 22273023 (Schillebeeckx 1992, doi:10.1016/0375-9474(92)90296-V) is digitised from Fig. 8 of the paper, and scatters: AH = 150, 156 and 158 lie 1.4 to 2.9 MeV above the mean of their neighbours. It is written unsmoothed, and the record gives the mass-integrated width it yields with the 239-Pu(nth,f) Y(A) and mean TKE of Wagemans 1984 against the 11.81 ± 0.01 MeV measured for 239-Pu(n_th,f) (22273003).

The joint yield Y(A, TKE)

yield against ["mass", "total_kinetic_energy"] is the joint pre-neutron distribution, one row per (A, TKE) cell. It is recorded event by event and published as counts, so it is admitted in arbitrary units and written under relative/, and the record states the TKE grid and the normalisation as the subentry gives them. The archive holds one for these four systems, 23268002 (Göök 2014, doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.90.064611), whose code names no TKE and whose csv rendering drops the TKE column; it is read from its subentry. The record adds two statements the data support: its TKE values are the centres of 1-MeV bins, since as centres the matrix reproduces the mean TKE of 23268004 to 0.05 MeV and as edges misses by 0.5 MeV; and its mass marginal is 23268003, to a relative 4 × 10⁻⁶.

For 240-Pu(sf) the archive holds the raw event counts of Demattè 1997 (22413013, doi:10.1016/S0375-9474(97)00032-8), coded MASS,PRE,FY/DE,,RAW: 1-MeV TKE steps from 140 to 210 MeV against heavy masses 120 to 160, from double-energy measurement corrected for prompt neutron emission. The code gives the energy as a fragment's (EN-SEC (E,FF)), but its range is that of the TKE; the subentry heads the counts NO-DIM, which the csv rendering turns into PC/FIS/MEV. It is read from its subentry as counts in arbitrary units.

The archive holds slices of the joint distribution besides — yields against mass at a few fixed energies of one fragment or of both, and TKE distributions for a few masses. They are not the distribution over the fragmentation range and are not retrieved as it; the record of each Y_vs_A_TKE retrieval lists those of its system under slices, with their masses and energies.

The mean neutron energy

neutron_kinetic_energy is the mean energy ⟨ε⟩ of the prompt neutrons of a fragment in the centre-of-mass frame of that fragment, coded MASS,PR,KE,N. It is retrieved against ["mass"], under eps_vs_A/, and against ["mass", "total_kinetic_energy"], under eps_vs_A_TKE/.

Most subentries head the value DATA-CM rather than DATA. For this ordinate alone that column is the datum, read with its uncertainty columns, and the csv rendering is compared with it row by row, to a relative 10⁻⁵ since the rendering writes six significant digits; see CENTRE_OF_MASS_ORDINATES. The frame is recorded per dataset as ordinate_frame, with ordinate_frame_basis and ordinate_frame_evidence (ordinate_frame). The basis is one of three: heading where the subentry heads the value DATA-CM; subentry where its text names the frame, as the REACTION text of 41502009 (Batenkov 2004) does; and publication where the subentry is silent and the publication names the frame. That publication is the one the subentry cites or, where that article was not obtained, the authors' paper of the same title and data, quoted in the evidence with its DOI (ORDINATE_FRAMES). Four datasets headed DATA are read from the publication, and all four are in the centre of mass:

  • 23175012 (Budtz-Jørgensen 1988, 252-Cf), by its article (doi:10.1016/0375-9474(88)90508-8): "The neutron energy η in the center-of-mass system of the fragment was evaluated event by event" (section 3.2.3, p. 322), and "Fig. 17a displays the average energy η as function of A" (p. 323).
  • 41689005 (Piksaykin 1977, 252-Cf), by the authors' paper of the same title at the Kiev conference of 1975 (Neitronnaya Fizika, part 5, p. 92, Moscow 1976), whose Fig. 4 is captioned as the mean energy of the neutrons in the centre-of-mass system against fragment mass. Its mean weights in the component isotropic in the laboratory, and the authors put about 10 % on it. The article, Yad. Fiz. 25, 723, was not obtained.
  • 14369005 (Fraser 1966, 233-U), by Fig. 5 of Milton and Fraser, Physics and Chemistry of Fission (Salzburg 1965), IAEA STI/PUB/101, vol. 2, p. 47, the figure the subentry digitises: "The symbol η is used for E_CM".
  • 22660004 (Nishio 1998, 233-U, against A and TKE), by Fig. 5 of doi:10.1080/18811248.1998.9733919: "Average neutron energy in the center-of-mass system as a function of total kinetic energy".

A dataset whose publication leaves the frame open, or for which none has been read, is recorded unstated, written, and flagged among its qualifiers by an entry beginning frame unstated: and in frame_warning under [datasets]; none of the shipped configurations now writes one. A value stated to be in the laboratory frame is refused, and no dataset of the shipped configurations is.

Each record also says how the mean was formed, as its publication states it (MEAN_FORMATIONS). mean_formed_from is measured_spectrum, the first moment of the measured centre-of-mass spectrum; fitted_spectrum, the first moment of a form fitted to it; completed_spectrum, the measured spectrum completed beyond its range by a fitted form; or unstated. mean_fitted_form gives the form where one enters, mean_threshold_mev and mean_threshold_frame the low-energy limit of the neutrons that enter the mean and the frame it is stated in, the detector threshold in the laboratory or the lower limit of the averaged centre-of-mass spectrum, and mean_evidence the sentence or equation, with the DOI. A threshold is recorded only from a publication read; what an EXFOR entry alone says of one is quoted in mean_evidence. Where a publication leaves open whether the range below the limit enters the mean, the dataset carries a qualifier beginning mean_threshold_unsettled: and the limit is not a cut.

The tag rule of neutron_kinetic_energy admits PRE in SF5. With N required in SF7 the code is none of the fragment energies, whose rules forbid N, and PRE beside PR marks the pre-neutron mass of the abscissa, as in MASS,PRE/PR/FRG,NU. 41689005 (Piksaykin 1977, 98-CF-252(0,F)MASS,PRE/PR,KE,N) is coded so: a mean neutron energy of 1.08 to 1.84 MeV against pre-neutron masses 92 to 160 on a 4-u grid, 18 lines of which 9 carry a statistical uncertainty, digitised from Fig. 2 of Yad. Fiz. 25, 723 (1977).

No reaction-code test can tell a fragment kinetic energy coded KE,N from a neutron energy; its magnitude can. A dataset holding more than MAXIMUM_NEUTRON_KINETIC_ENERGY, 5 MeV, on any row is refused, the reason giving the number of rows above the bound and the largest value. An evaporation spectrum ε exp(−ε/T) has the mean energy 2T (Weisskopf, doi:10.1103/PhysRev.52.295), so 5 MeV asks for T = 2.5 MeV, where the temperatures fitted to the measured spectra stay below 1.4 MeV. The largest mean the archive holds for these systems is 3.68 ± 0.64 MeV, at A = 180 in 23268011 (Göök 2014), and every other lies below 3.3 MeV; no fragment carries less than about 40 MeV. 23164022 (Al-Adili 2016, 92-U-235(N,F)MASS,PRE/PR,KE,N) holds fragment kinetic energies, 43.6 to 101.9 MeV on all 94 rows, and is refused on the bound.

Against ["total_kinetic_energy"] alone the archive holds nothing for the five systems: the four datasets coded KE/TKE,N all carry MASS, and no eps_vs_TKE configuration is shipped. The joint ⟨ε⟩(A, TKE) is retrieved for 252-Cf and 233-U; 235-U, in either channel, and 239-Pu have no eps_vs_A_TKE configuration.

The parameters of the fitted spectrum

neutron_spectrum_temperature and neutron_spectrum_exponent are the effective temperature Teff and the exponent λ of the form const η^λ exp(−η/Teff) fitted to the centre-of-mass spectrum of the neutrons of a fragment of given mass. Against ["mass"], Teff is written under `TeffvsA/asA Teff Teffuncertaintyin MeV, and λ, a pure number, underlambdavsA/asA lambda lambdauncertainty. EXFOR has no code for either; they sit inMISCcolumns of the mean neutron energy. Like the width of the TKE distribution they are therefore read only from the columns a configuration names, one[[fitparameter]]table per dataset with the keyssubentryandcolumn(see [Configurations](configurations.md)), and the uncertainty is the column<column>-ERR([PARAMETERORDINATES](@ref)). The dataset is selected as the mean neutron energy is, by the same tag rule, the 5 MeV bound and the frame. The record gives, per dataset,parametercolumn,parameterunitandparameteris, and the form fitted with the equation of the publication asfitformandfitevidence([SPECTRUMFITS`](@ref)).

Cf252_sf_T_eff_vs_A reads MISC1 of 23175012 (Budtz-Jørgensen 1988), 79 masses with Teff from 0.77 to 1.39 MeV, and MISC of 23268011 (Göök 2014), 82 masses of its 109, from 0.72 to 1.40 MeV; `Cf252sflambdavsAreadsMISC2of23175012`, 79 masses with λ from 0.11 to 0.94. Budtz-Jørgensen fits Eq. (8) of the article, the cascade evaporation spectrum of Le Couteur and Lang, with λ and the temperature free; the subentry calls the same form a Weisskopf spectrum. Göök 2014 fits Eq. (14) of doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.90.064611, φ(η) ∝ η^λ exp(−η/Teff), and its λ, Fig. 18c, is not in the archive. The archive holds no such column for the other systems.

Spectrum ratios

Under spectrum the archive holds four 252-Cf(sf) datasets as N(E)/√E, RRE among the modifiers of their code: 23175004, 40064027, 40250003 and 41689002. They are written as tabulated, not converted, and carry a qualifier beginning spectrum_form:. It holds one evaluation there, V0101002 (Mannhart 1987), written and flagged with a qualifier beginning evaluation:.

The archive gives the spectrum as a ratio in two forms, and each is an ordinate of its own.

spectrum_maxwellian_ratio is the spectrum divided by a Maxwellian √E exp(−E/T), coded with MXD, which spectrum excludes. Every dataset must state the temperature it was formed with: the column KT-NRM of the COMMON section of its subentry, else of subentry 001, else a constant column of its DATA table, in an energy unit. It is recorded as maxwellian_temperature_mev with maxwellian_temperature_source (maxwellian_temperature), and a dataset that gives none is refused. 14278003 (Poenitz 1982, 252-Cf) holds in KT-NRM the mean energy of the Maxwellian, 2.159 MeV, as its ANALYSIS text says, and the record gives T = 1.439 MeV, two thirds of it. The ratio form is retrieved for 252-Cf(sf) and for thermal 235-U, 239-Pu and 233-U. For each of the three thermal systems Starostov 1985 (entry 40930) gives the ratio in four datasets, the first measurement cycle with an anthracene crystal, a stilbene crystal and a plastic scintillator, and the second cycle. Their subentries name each other under STATUS with COREL; they are written separately and marked correlated_with, one measurement for any combination. Their relation is complementary_range, the second cycle repeating the lower range and extending it downwards.

spectrum_cf252_ratio is the ratio of the prompt fission neutron spectrum of the system and that of 252-Cf(sf), both at the same outgoing neutron energy, tabulated against ["neutron_energy"] only. Of the shape data it is the least dependent on the detector efficiency, which cancels. It is the one observable read from a combination of reaction codes (spectrum_ratio): the code must be the ratio of exactly two reactions, one the system's and one 98-CF-252(0,F), each satisfying the rule of spectrum — so no MXD, no MSC, no angle-differential or partial spectrum — with no variable headed for numerator or denominator alone (-NM, -DN). Sums, differences, products, ratios of ratios and ratios between two other systems stay refused, under this ordinate and every other, and the ordinate is refused for 252-Cf(sf) itself.

The archive gives both orientations. Values are written as tabulated, never inverted, and the record gives per dataset ratio_orientation, system_over_reference or reference_over_system, with ratio_numerator and ratio_denominator, and the qualifiers of both reactions; orientation_warning under [datasets] says when a directory holds both. Datasets in arbitrary units go under relative/, as for spectra. Ratios coded with MSC are refused: 10911002, 10911003 and 10911004 (Smith 1980) hold the logarithm of the ratio in arbitrary units at 525 keV, and 41502002, 41502003 and 41502004 (Batenkov 2004) were plotted on a scale their author states was probably logarithmic.

The multiplicity distribution

multiplicity_distribution is the probability P(ν) of emitting ν neutrons in a fission, tabulated against ["neutron_number"] and written under P_vs_nu/ as nu P P_uncertainty. The two go together and with nothing else, and the loader refuses any other pairing. The quantity code is NU, and the archive marks the distribution with the branch NUM: ,NUM,NU, ,PR/NUM,NU, once NPART,NUM,NU. The subentry tabulates it against PART-OUT, the number of outgoing particles, which the csv rendering does not carry. The rows of the subentry and of the rendering are aligned by order and the probabilities compared line by line.

The rule asks for NUM in SF5 and NU in SF6, and refuses a distribution for a fragment mass or charge (MASS, ELEM) and one derived from a model (DERIV: 30544002, a simulated binomial distribution). An evaluation (EVAL: V0045011 to V0045013, Holden 1988) is written and flagged with a qualifier beginning evaluation:, with evaluation_warning under [datasets], like every other statement of the archive on the standing of a dataset. Arbitrary units are refused: 14064003 holds counts, and 23598004 distributions for the fragment charges 42 and 56.

The data decide what a distribution is. The archive codes the distribution of the neutrons detected alike, and its mean is ν̄ times the detection efficiency, so a distribution whose mean lies further than a quarter of ν̄ from ν̄ is refused (MEAN_MULTIPLICITY_BAND): 10300005 (Balagna 1973, mean 2.48, 0.66 ν̄), 10930004 (Halperin 1980, 1.65, 0.44 ν̄) and 14064002 (Hicks 1955, 1.43, 0.38 ν̄), all of 252-Cf. ν̄ is the total ν̄ of the IAEA neutron data standards 2017 (doi:10.1016/j.nds.2018.02.002; NUBAR_STANDARDS): 3.764 ± 0.016 for 252-Cf, 2.425 ± 0.011 for 235-U, 2.878 ± 0.013 for 239-Pu and 2.487 ± 0.011 for 233-U. It includes the delayed neutrons, 0.2 to 0.7 % of it.

A table is written as tabulated, never renormalised. The record gives per dataset what it implies, and applies none of it (distribution_moments): distribution_sum and distribution_normalised, whether the sum lies within 0.01 of one; mean_multiplicity, Σ ν P / Σ P, with mean_multiplicity_uncertainty where every line states an uncertainty, the lines taken as uncorrelated; mean_nubar and mean_nubar_uncertainty; mean_deviation, mean/ν̄ − 1, with mean_deviation_uncertainty; and mean_consistent, whether the deviation lies within three standard deviations of zero. Every accepted mean is consistent with ν̄ within three standard deviations where an uncertainty is stated.

Post-neutron products

Three observables describe the fission products after prompt neutron emission, each against one abscissa, and each is retrieved for the four systems: <system>_Y_vs_A_p, <system>_Y_vs_Z_A_p and <system>_E_K_p_vs_A_p. As for every observable, the qualifiers MXW and SPA are recorded under nth and not refused, while FIS, EPI and FST reject the dataset.

The post-neutron mass yield

yield against ["product_mass"] is the mass yield of the products, MASS,SEC,FY or MASS,CHN,FY, written under Y_vs_A_p/ as A_p Y Y_uncertainty. The abscissa requires MASS in SF4 and SEC, IND or CHN in SF5, and refuses PRE, ELEM, TKE and DE. A chain yield, CHN, is the yield of a mass chain after beta decay, which conserves A, so it is the post-neutron mass yield up to the delayed-neutron transfer between neighbouring chains, below a per cent. It is written as one and flagged: its qualifiers carry an entry beginning CHN: chain yield: with that reading, scale_warning under [datasets] names it, and the record states the reading under [conventions] as chain_yield. The pre-neutron yield against mass and the independent yield of a nuclide refuse CHN. A blank branch establishes nothing and is refused, and the provisional yield PRV is not among the alternatives. CUM, the cumulative yield, is refused for every observable, and the partial branch PAR as for the independent yield below.

The csv rendering restates the unit of the subentry, PC/FIS as PART/FIS with the values divided by 100, and the values written are the rendering's: a mass yield in PART/FIS sums to 2 over both fragments. The normalisation statement of the record follows the unit the file is written in, and unit_subentry records the unit the subentry heads the datum with. Both hold for every yield, the pre-neutron ones included.

Every yield carries yield_sum, the sum of the written yields of the dataset in the unit the file is written in: for a mass yield in PART/FIS, 2 over both fragments and about 1 over one side. It is recorded and never a cut, and the convention yield_sum under [conventions] states it for every yield retrieval. A sum its coverage does not explain is to be read with the publication: 31738002 (Koblik 2014, MASS,SEC,FY,,SPA) sums to 1.82 over the heavy group alone, A_p 125 to 155, and is written as the archive gives it.

The independent yield

yield against ["charge", "product_mass"] is the independent yield of a nuclide, ELEM/MASS,IND,FY, written under Y_vs_Z_A_p/ as Z A_p Y Y_uncertainty. The abscissa requires MASS and ELEM in SF4 and SEC or IND in SF5, and refuses PRE, KE, DE and CRN.

ELEM is a code of the product subfield of the reaction code and never a heading of the DATA table, which heads the atomic number of a nuclide ELEMENT (EXFOR Dictionary 24), its mass number MASS and its isomeric state ISOMER. The csv rendering codes the product as 1000·Z + A, ProdZA, and the isomer as ProdM, and the two tables are aligned row by row.

A subentry of one element may hold ELEMENT, and at times MASS or ISOMER, as a constant of its COMMON section or of the COMMON section of subentry 001 of the entry. With the charge in the abscissa such a constant is placed beside the lines of the DATA table before the tables are compared, and a rendering that then codes the product as a bare mass is accepted. The record lists the constants used as common_constants, ELEMENT = 51.0 [NO-DIM] for instance. Twenty-one datasets of 235-U, nine of 239-Pu, five of 233-U and one of 252-Cf are read so, among them 12740002 (Rengan 1982), 20848003 (Fujiwara 1982), 21218004 (Kaiser 1978), 21641002 (Balestrini 1979), 23709002 (Strom 1965), 30912002 (Engler 1983), 30953004 (Ehrenberg 1972), 30960002 (Shmid

  1. and 21706004 (Zwicky 1980).

A nuclide reported in several isomeric rows is written once. Where the archive gives its own total, the row without an isomer mark, that row is written; otherwise the rows sharing one mark are repeat measurements of that state and are combined as repeats, by the inverse-variance weighted mean, and the states so resolved are summed with their uncertainties in quadrature. 23709002 (Strom 1965) gives the ground-state yield of Sb-124 five times, four samples and their average under FLAG, and 33158004 (Naik 2021) the isomeric yield of I-136 through two decay lines; both are combined, not summed. Such combinations count in abscissae_combined, and combined_over names the auxiliary columns that varied, FLAG and DECAY-FLAG there.

A nuclide given in one marked state alone is written as it stands and is the yield of that state, not of the nuclide. Several rows without a mark are totals that cannot be told apart, and are combined as repeats. The record names the nuclides of each dataset, by labels such as Sb-130, in isomer_summed_nuclides, isomer_single_state_nuclides and isomer_ambiguous_nuclides, beside the counts isomer_totals_used, isomer_states_summed and isomer_groups_ambiguous; it states the convention as isomers under [conventions], and isomer_warning under [datasets] lists the datasets concerned.

Independent yields coded with the nuclide in the reaction code, 55-CS-136,IND,FY and the like, one nuclide per dataset and no ELEMENT or MASS table, are not retrieved: they carry neither MASS nor ELEM in SF4.

Four codes file under ELEM/MASS what is not the independent yield. The ordinate yield refuses FRC (Dictionary 34, fractional), the independent yield as a fraction of its chain yield, which is a ratio and not the yield: under this abscissa 39 datasets of 235-U, 15 of 239-Pu, 11 of 233-U and 5 of 252-Cf, 23558005 (Günther 1972) and 14088003 to 14088006 for instance. The abscissa refuses CRN (Dictionary 31, "more than two fragments are specified"), the yields of correlated fragment pairs, tabulated against ELEM1/MASS1 and ELEM2/MASS2: 15 such datasets for 252-Cf, 13648002 to 13648006, 13749002 and 14634002 among them. The two post-neutron yields refuse the partial branch PAR (Dictionary 31): 21689014 to 21689018 (Lang 1980, Lohengrin) each hold the yields at one product kinetic energy, a constant E of their COMMON section, 88.5 MeV in the first, and are slices of the energy-resolved distribution; the same entry gives the independent yield as 21689019, which is written. The pre-neutron yield against mass is not concerned. CUM, the cumulative yield, is refused for every observable.

The archive holds 9 for 252-Cf, 52 for 235-U, 24 for 239-Pu and 18 for 233-U. The large tables are, for 235-U, 10722003 (Strittmatter 1977, 100 nuclides, A_p 82 to 106), 21689019 (Lang 1980, 113 nuclides, 80 to 107), 221610021 (Rudstam 1990, 92 nuclides written, about a quarter of them summed over isomeric states), 22151003 (Sida 1989, 70 to 84), 23586004 (Armbruster 1987, 70 to 80), 23558007 (Günther 1972, 130 to 140) and 13600002 (Tipnis 1998); for 239-Pu, 23558006 (Günther 1972, 130 to 139) and 22985008 (Bail 2009, 58 nuclides); and for 233-U, 10918003 (Wehring 1980, 100 nuclides) and 23794002 (Johansson 1997, 157 nuclides). The rest are radiochemical tables of a few nuclides each.

The post-neutron kinetic energy

product_kinetic_energy against ["product_mass"] is the kinetic energy of a product of given mass after neutron emission, MASS,SEC,KE, AKE accepted as KE. It is written under E_K_p_vs_A_p/ as A_p E_K_p E_K_p_uncertainty in MeV, restated from the EV of the rendering. The ordinate composes with the product-mass abscissa: KE in SF6, MASS in SF4 and SEC or IND in SF5, and no LF+HF, no N, no PRE and no ELEM; the chain branch CHN, which the abscissa admits for a yield, names a yield and is refused here.

For 252-Cf the archive holds no MASS,SEC,KE: its post-neutron kinetic energies, 32928002 and 32928003, are ELEM,SEC,AKE, resolved in charge, and Cf252_sf_E_K_p_vs_A_p writes nothing. For 235-U it holds nine: 21543017 (Asghar 1977), 21313014 (Asghar 1978), 21752017 (Wagemans 1981), 21981015 (Geltenbort 1985), 23551003 and 23551004 (Konecny 1970), the second combined over MISC1 to MISC3, which hold cooling times, 235580032 (Günther 1972), 31797003 (Bakhromi 1982) and 41496005 (Aleksandrov 1994), interpolated from non-integer masses. For 239-Pu five: 21544009 (Asghar 1978), 21981016 (Geltenbort 1985), 235580022 (Günther 1972), 23583003 (Boucheneb 1991) and 416930051 (Belyaev 1988). For 233-U two: 21981014 (Geltenbort 1985) and 23784003 (Chebboubi 2021).

Prompt γ rays

The archive codes prompt γ rays with the photon as the product, 0-G-0, and returns them under the quantities FY and E: the multiplicity per fission as 0-G-0,PR,FY (the photons per fission of LEXFOR, Multiplicity), the energies as 0-G-0,PR,KE, and the values of one fragment against its mass with G as the particle considered, MASS,PRE/PR/FRG,FY,G. The γ codes stay refused for every other ordinate.

Against a mass, two quantities are retrieved. M_gamma_bar_vs_A and E_gamma_tot_vs_A hold a quantity per fission, M̄γ(A) and Eγ,tot(A): the photons of both fragments of the fissions in which one fragment has the mass A, as Hotzel 1996, Singer 1997 and Mehta 1973 tabulate them. M_gamma_vs_A and E_gamma_vs_A hold the quantity of one fragment, Mγ(A) and Eγ(A): the photons of the fragment of mass A alone, as Pleasonton 1972 and Krupa 2004 give them. The per-fission value of a pair is the sum of the per-fragment values of its two fragments, so the two tables do not compare row for row.

A quantity per fission is written one value to a file. A dataset giving several, two detectors of one experiment in one table as Qi 2018 does, is refused with its values rather than combined into one no publication states, and a dataset tabulated over bins of γ energy, as Verbinski 1973 is, is refused as tabulated against them.

Conventions. Five decisions that no code of the archive implies; the source and the records cite them by number. (1) M_gamma_bar_vs_A and eps_gamma_vs_A are retrieved and hold quantities per fission, the photons of both fragments of the fissions in which one fragment has the mass A; M_gamma_vs_A and E_gamma_vs_A hold the quantities of that fragment alone; a dataset enters one of the two by what its entry states, never both. (2) Skarsvåg 1970 (23174004), the multiplicity per fission above 100 keV, is admitted by name for M_gamma_bar, with listed_under = "MLT", although the archive lists it under the quantity MLT alone, which no configuration queries; nothing else comes in from MLT. (3) The totals of Smith 1956 (14320004) and Sokol 1991 (415800031) and the mean photon energy of Krupa 2004 (23814004), stated in auxiliary columns with no uncertainty beside them, are never written as datasets; they are kept, with their words and their place, in a reason among the rejections of the observable they would answer. (4) The ∼5 ns window of Pleasonton applies to the 235-U sets of 14214 and 14197 on the strength of the abstract of 1972 and to the 233-U and 239-Pu sets of 14214 on the strength of the abstract of 1973; the 252-Cf sets of that report stay unstated. (5) The multiplicity of one fragment at A = 82 in 14197005, −0.36 ± 1.36, is written as the archive gives it, below zero and compatible with it, and row_notes says so; dropping it would bias the row.

What defines a value. The total γ energy per fission differs between experiments by up to about 1 MeV on its definition alone: the γ-energy range the photons were counted over, the time window after fission, and the corrections made for what lies outside either. Each dataset records them as read and is never cut on them. The range comes from the headings E-MIN and E-MAX of the entry, in its DATA table or a COMMON section, where they state one; a range, a window, a time resolution or a correction stated in the text of the entry, or in the publication it cites, is a curated statement, verbatim, looked for in the text at every retrieval and recorded with the place it stands. gamma_definition_warning names the datasets whose range or window nobody states, as the 1972 report of Pleasonton (14214) states neither for any of its four systems; convention (4) gives the windows its abstracts supply. Gatera 2017 and the four detectors of Oberstedt 2015 state a prompt gate of ±3 ns in their publications; the 300 ns acquisition trigger that the entry of Gatera states is recorded beside the gate as acquisition_window_ns. A set the archive codes partial, PR/PAR over a stated range, is written beside the full-range sets and named in gamma_range_warning: Makii 2019 counts 2.29 photons per fission over 1 to 6 MeV, a third of the full multiplicity. So is a set whose stated range is a threshold or a ceiling of the detection, gamma_range_limited: Sokol 1991 counted photons of 320 to 5120 keV, and Wright 2024 those of 0.8 to 6.8 MeV, the threshold of its NaI detectors forced to 0.8 MeV by the γ-flash of n_TOF.

Which energy. The archive codes the total energy per fission and the mean photon energy alike, 0-G-0,PR,KE: 14214029 holds 0.85 MeV per photon and 143870062 9.0 MeV per fission under the same code, and MSC marks the total in one entry and the mean in another. The magnitude decides, the total lying between 3 and 15 MeV and the mean between 0.3 and 2.5 MeV, and the REACTION text is the basis where it names the same quantity ("Total gammas energy.", "Average gamma energy per gamma"); a text naming the other refuses the dataset, and a reading on magnitude alone is named in quantity_reading_warning. Oberstedt 2013 (235-U), Gatera 2017 (239-Pu) and Makii 2019 state the total γ energy per fission with its uncertainty in the additional results of their entries and in no DATA table. Not covered

The centre-of-mass spectrum Φ(ε) is not retrieved. The archive does not carry it as a quantity of its own, so a measurement in the centre of mass is compiled under the same reaction code as a laboratory-frame one. The subentry heads its energy column E-CM, and 23268009 (Göök, 2014), such a dataset, is rejected by Cf252_sf_spectrum_vs_E on that heading rather than retrieved alongside laboratory spectra. The refusal concerns spectra: the mean neutron energy is read from DATA-CM where its subentry heads it so.

The prompt fission γ-ray spectrum (0-G-0,PR,FY/DE), the distribution of the γ multiplicity (0-G-0,NUM,FY) and the γ values of one fragment group, light or heavy, are not retrieved; the last are kept in the record of the dataset that gives them; see Prompt γ rays.

P(ν) of the fission is retrieved, as multiplicity_distribution; P(ν) per fragment and the variance of ν against mass or TKE are not.

Ratios of the spectra of two neutron-induced systems, (A(n,f),PR,NU/DE)/(B(n,f),PR,NU/DE), of which the archive holds many, are a distinct observable and are not retrieved. The ratios to the spectrum of 252-Cf(sf) and to a Maxwellian are retrieved, as spectrum_cf252_ratio and spectrum_maxwellian_ratio.

Relative data

A prompt fission neutron spectrum is conventionally measured relative and normalised afterwards, so for 235-U(n,f) most of what the archive holds is in arbitrary units: of the 125 datasets offered under MFQ, 42 answer the query in arbitrary units against 15 in absolute ones, and a thermal window narrows both to 11 and 6, of which 23764007 is refused for its centre-of-mass frame. The rest are retrieved, and written under relative/ rather than beside the absolute ones:

data/U235_nth/spectrum_vs_E/
├── *.dat        # absolute, PC/FIS/MEV or 1/EV
├── relative/    # arbitrary units — a shape, with no scale
├── subentries/
└── retrieval.toml

A relative dataset cannot be put on a common scale with any other, relative or absolute: each has to be normalised on its own before it is compared, and none may be averaged with absolute data. Because they are kept apart, a reader that takes a whole directory cannot pick one up by accident. The run record marks each accepted dataset relative = true or false and names them all in one warning.

This applies to the spectrum ordinates and to the joint yield Y(A, TKE) alone. A joint yield is recorded event by event and published as counts on a grid, and what it carries is the TKE distribution at each mass, which a consumer normalises to a mass yield. Every other observable refuses arbitrary units, since a relative value there cannot be interpreted: a one-dimensional Y(A) in counts has no scale to read off, a kinetic energy in arbitrary units is not an energy, and the scale of a multiplicity is the whole quantity.
